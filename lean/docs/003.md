## Scope

**003a (no read-only comparator).** An open network has arbitrary finite arc and internal-vertex types, integral lower and upper bounds, integral (possibly negative) costs and fixed integral internal excesses. Its boundary function `Φ_G(b)` is defined as the least cost of a feasible integral flow with boundary `b` (`⊤` if there is none). The formalization proves the boundary exchange theorem: `Φ_G` satisfies the M-convex exchange axiom for every network with nonempty domain. It also proves the flow-level exchange behind it, by a conformal path-extraction lemma. Using only the exchange theorem, it proves that no network is a pass-through gadget, with context-independence condition (ii), whose table vanishes on the two all-equal states and is positive on every proper nonempty set of channels (no all-equal sensor, any `r ≥ 2`). In particular no network is a read-only comparator with table `|x − y|`. The crossbar remark is proved: the four-arc crossbar realizes `|x − y|` on the intended states, but its two crossed states cost `0`. The one-shot comparator identity is proved. So is the theorem that no submodular function on visible and auxiliary Boolean labels has the NAND relation, or any of its eight complemented versions, as its projected minimizer relation. The statement that graph-cut energies are submodular is not formalized, and neither is the "no local bridge" corollary as a separate statement. That corollary is the all-equal-sensor theorem applied to the residual program.

**003b (fractional covers).** Carrier graphs are finite directed graphs with fusion pairs. Paths are nonempty arc chains, and weights are taken over the vertex set of a path. Only weak duality is formalized for the path-flow dual: every dual-feasible pair `(y, λ)` gives `∑ y_e ≤ d` for every primal-feasible `(x, d)`. Strong duality and the node-split flow form are not formalized. The two-path concentration theorem is proved as an exact finite averaging statement: some two paths in the support of `λ` are crossed by at least `D²/s` pairs, and some two paths carry crossing weight at least `D³/s²`. The incomparable case, with factor two, assumes that no support path contains both endpoints of a support pair. The factor-two example is formalized. For two unary rails with readouts containing a perfect matching, the formalization proves that the fractional catalyst load is exactly `h/2`, with matching primal and lower bounds. This covers both the matched-rail mirage and the complete readout grid. For the mirage, the least catalyst height is proved to be `⌈h/2⌉`. Not formalized: the matching and ladder corollaries (Lemma 4.1 and Corollaries 4.5–4.6, including the Erdős–Szekeres step), the `O(log h)` evaluation depth of the mirage and the conditional exponent arithmetic of Section 6.

**003c (flow reductions and RNC).** The main theorems of this manuscript are conditional parallel-complexity statements, and they are not formalized: the randomized flow transfer, the RNC transfer, NC verification and the PRAM cost of the Las Vegas conversion. The formalized parts are only the exact mathematical facts behind them. The first is cost-independent conservation (Proposition 7.1): the net outflows of all vertices sum to zero, and a gadget with no internal supplies has external outflow equal to external inflow. The second is the expectation `∑_{k≥1} k p (1−p)^{k−1} = 1/p` and the total probability `1` behind Corollary 6.2. The third is the exponent arithmetic of Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 (`√(S^α)·S^λ = S^{α/2+λ}`). The fourth is the three inequalities of the calibration proposition 4.4.

**003d (equal-flow programs).** Monotone fan-in-two circuits are given in topological order on `Fin n`, and their values are defined by recursion. The NAND normalization of alternating circuits is proved. So is the NAND polytope theorem: the convex hull in `ℝ³` of the four NAND triples equals the polytope cut out by `z + x ≥ 1`, `z + y ≥ 1`, `z + x + y ≤ 2` and the unit cube. The forcing lemma is proved. The residual NAND program is defined explicitly, with a star spanning tree on the occurrences of each gate; the paper allows any spanning tree. For alternating circuits the formalization proves items (iv) and (v) of the dyadic elimination theorem. The transcript point is feasible with cost `0`, every feasible point has cost `≥ 0`, and every feasible cost-`0` point equals the transcript point on all program variables. The hard-equality form is proved as well: the feasible point is unique and integral, and each gate's occurrences carry `b_u`. The cut-size bound is proved together with the weak max-flow/min-cut inequality it uses. Not formalized: the size bound and the coefficient bookkeeping (i)–(iii) of Theorem 4.2, which hold by inspection of the definition. Also not formalized: the explicit decomposition of Theorem 5.1 into `3g` two-vertex networks with capacities and supplies at most two, and logarithmic-space computability.

**003e (catalyst height).** Covers, heights and fractional certificates are defined on carrier graphs. The factor-two rounding theorem is proved: the threshold set is a catalyst cover meeting every path in at most `2d` nodes, and a cover of height `k` gives a fractional certificate of value `k`. Tightness is proved on one fusion of two sources (`d* = 1/2`, `χ = 1`). Only the "if" direction of the path-potential lemma is formalized. The core of the catalyst-height compiler is formalized as a combinatorial statement. Take a cover and any grading of it that strictly increases along proper ancestry, such as the levels `ℓ_H`. Then in round `r` every node of the cone `C_r` has at most one predecessor not yet known. Relative to the cover, every node has at most one unknown predecessor (joint composition). Two examples are also proved: a cover made of sources has height one (the accumulator), and every cover of the two-register recurrence of length `m` has height at least `m`. The PRAM depth bounds (`O((cht+1)log² n)`, functional closure in `O(log n)`) and NC checkability are not formalized.

**003f (circuit LPs).** The circuit LP of a monotone circuit is proved to have exactly one feasible point, the value vector. The Fibonacci OR-ladder point is proved for `n ≥ 2`: `x̃_0 = x̃_1 = 0`, `x̃_n = 1/2`, the box and lower OR constraints hold, and every upper OR constraint is violated by exactly `δ = 1/(2(F_{n+1}−1))`. It is also proved that `1/(2φ^n) ≤ δ ≤ φ^{-(n-1)}`. For the ladder matrix `T_m`, `det T_m = 1`, and the minor deleting the first row and last column has absolute value `F_m`. Simultaneous escape switching (Theorem 5.2 (i), (ii), (iv), (v)) is proved. So is the rank-biased bound. Start from a child of maximum Strahler rank at every OR gate. After `k ≥ sr(v)` rounds every true vertex `v` is outside the losing set. If all ranks are `≤ r`, then `L_r` is exactly the set of false vertices and there is no escape. Not formalized: the Latin-component contraction and the low-cyclomatic-excess compiler of Sections 2–3, the Hadamard upper bound, the rank-layer reachability lemma, and all PRAM depth claims.
