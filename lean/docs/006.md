## Scope

All files are in `GC/Computability/SeqNORNormalForms/`. A parent array of length $n$ is a function $j:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ with $j_i<i$ for $1\le i\le n$, and its trace is $x_0=1$, $x_i=\mathrm{NOR}(x_{i-1},x_{j_i})$. Complexity classes (L, NC, P, quasiNC, PolyL), logspace reductions, circuit size and circuit depth are not formalized anywhere. What is formalized is the mathematical content of the constructions: their correctness, their combinatorial invariants, and the round counts of explicitly defined iterations.

**006a (Two pages).** We define alternating-direction NOR/OR arrays (Definition 7.1) with the six cell types and their sources. We define the serpentine instance $\Phi$ of Section 7.4 exactly as in the paper: constant blocks, one 8-position block per cell (Table 2) at $c(t,s)=2+2k+8(k(t-1)+e)$, and the tail. We prove that $\Phi$ is a parent array, that $x_{o(t,s)}=v(t,s)$ for every cell and $x_n=v(d,s_{\mathrm{out}})$ (Lemmas 7.6 and 7.7), and that the paper's explicit page assignment is a valid two-page assignment, so $\mathrm{pg}(\Phi)\le2$ (Lemmas 7.8–7.10, Theorem 7.11). The slot programs of Lemma 7.3 and the three-column gadgets of Table 1 are also formalized. We prove the gadget values and the claim that no cell reads outside its three columns (Lemma 7.4). We prove that the array built from a NOR circuit computes $w_G$ at cell $(d,2G)$ (Lemma 7.5). Together these give the end-to-end statement: for every NOR circuit, the resulting parent array has page number at most 2 and last trace bit $w_G$. Also formalized: Okhotin's five-cell reduction (Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.3), the key step of Corollary 3.4 (with the dummy gate, $j_i\ne i-1$ for all $i\ge2$), Lemma 2.1, and the algebraic part of Proposition 7.2 (parity normalization turns NOR-only arrays into monotone OR/AND circuits). Of Proposition 7.2 we also prove the order-preserving layout of the wires. Not formalized: the logspace computability of all maps, the P-completeness and NC conclusions, planarity as a graph-theoretic notion and the Delcher–Kosaraju theorem, the instruction count $O(G^2)$, Proposition 5.1 (NP-completeness of $\mathrm{pg}\le k$), the one-page results of Section 6, and the live-width bound of Section 4.

**006b (Reset normal form).** Reset sets are represented by their indicator on positive positions, with the virtual reset $-1$ built into $\mathrm{pred}_R$. Formalized: the reset-predecessor normal form (Theorem 3.1), the event-queue form (Proposition 3.2), and the fact that $R^\star$ is a fixed point of the reset operator $\Phi$ and its unique one (Proposition 3.3, mathematical part). Also formalized: the first-disagreement lemma (Lemma 3.4), $R_t=R^\star$ for all $t\ge n$ (Corollary 3.5), the non-monotonicity example for $\Phi^2$ (Remark 3.6(b), checked by `decide` on the stated instance), and realizable traces (Theorem 3.7, the construction only, not the Fibonacci count). Not formalized: Borodin's theorem and Corollary 2.3, the $\mathrm{NC}^1$ circuit bounds of Proposition 3.3, the compilers of Section 4, the barriers of Section 5, and the computed (unproved) round count of Remark 3.6(a).

**006c (Square-root space).** Formalized: Theorem 6.1 in three parts. We construct the instance with reset set $\{1\}\cup S$. We prove that the reset sets of length-$n$ instances are exactly the subsets of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ containing $1$, and that there are $2^{n-1}$ of them. Also formalized: the counterexample of Section 4 to the source's embedding claim (no Sequential NOR graph has two vertices with the same two distinct in-neighbours), the subdivision embedding of Lemma 4.3, and the fan-out normalization of Theorem 5.1. For Theorem 5.1 the construction is written with explicit indices. We prove that it is a parent array of length $S_n+3n-1\le 3n^2+2n+1$ with the same output bit, in which every position is a parent at most twice. The lengths $4,12,23,37,54,74,97$ for $n\le7$ are checked. Not formalized: the time bound $O(n\log^3n)$ and the space bound $O(\sqrt n\log^2n)$ (Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.3), Williams' simulation, the pebbling results (Lemma 4.2, Theorem 4.4), the easy floor, Corollaries 6.2 and 6.3, and Section 2.

**006d (First-collision law).** Newton's method over $\mathbb F_2$ is defined explicitly. The Newton direction is computed by forward substitution, and we prove that it solves $J(x)d=F(x)$. Formalized: the exact error identity $J(x)e^+=q(e)$ (Lemma 2.1) and the first-collision law (Theorem 2.2): $e^+$ vanishes up to the first collision and equals $q$ there, and the first error advances by at least two. Also formalized: Corollary 2.3. From every start with $x_0=1$, Newton's method is exact on $0,\dots,n$ after $\lceil n/2\rceil$ rounds, and after $\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ rounds from $(1,0,\dots,0)$. Proposition 2.5 is checked on its instance. Not formalized: the contraction onto mixers (Theorem 2.4), the coefficient formula (Proposition 2.6), the tightness family (Theorem 2.7, so the lower bound $\lceil n/2\rceil$ is not formalized), and Section 3.

**006e (Blockwise guessing).** Formalized: bad shields and ranks (Definition 2.1), the candidate $Y(\gamma)$, prefix locality (Lemma 4.1) and true-prefix recovery (Corollary 4.2). The stage of Section 5 is defined explicitly, with branches, padding and validity bits, and the selection $\beta_{a+t}=\bigvee_\alpha(V_\alpha\wedge\alpha_t)$. We prove the unique-valid-branch lemma (Lemma 5.1). We also prove correctness of the schedule (Theorem 5.2, correctness part): on the promise $|H(j)|\le r$, after $\lceil r/g\rceil$ stages the recovered bits are the true bad-shield values and $Y(\beta)$ is the trace. Not formalized: circuit size and depth bounds, Corollaries 5.3 and 5.4, the pointer-doubling evaluator (Proposition 3.1), uniformity, and the abstract lifting theorem (Theorem 7.1).

**006f (Binary core).** Core variables are indexed by their core position rather than by their rank, an order-preserving renaming. Formalized: the root classification (Lemma 2.2), the descriptor equation $x_q=[\![\delta(q)]\!](z)$, triangularity and the unique solution of the core system (Definition 3.1, Lemma 3.2), and the mathematical content of the retraction theorem (Theorem 3.4): substituting any solution of the core system into the descriptors gives the trace. Also formalized: four-coordinate universality (Theorem 5.2). For every ordered NOR circuit, $\mathrm{Embed}(\mathcal C)$ is a parent array of length $1+2t+4G$ with $x_{\mathrm{Rep}(y)}=y$, its binary core is exactly $\{\mathrm{Rep}(w_i)\}$, and its core equations are those of $\mathcal C$. Not formalized: the circuit families $\mathrm{Core}_n$ and $\mathrm{Decode}_n$ and their depth, pointer doubling (Lemma 3.3), Section 4, logspace computability, and Corollaries 5.3–5.5 and Proposition 5.6.

**006g (Few long paths).** For a finite digraph given by an adjacency relation, directed paths are chains of vertices. Formalized: the path-hitting identity (Theorem 4.1) and the packing sandwich $q\le\delta_M\le(M+1)q$ (Theorem 4.3). Also formalized: the path formula $\delta_M(C_L)=\lfloor L/(M+1)\rfloor$ (Theorem 5.2). Not formalized: the bounded search tree algorithm and its running time (Theorem 4.2), the circuit compilers (Theorems 3.1, 6.2, 7.1, Corollary 4.4), Lemma 5.1 and Proposition 5.4 (the contracted DAG of the path family and of the triangle example), and the separator results.
