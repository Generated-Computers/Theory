## Scope

All files are in `GC/GroupTheory/A5Programs/`, namespace `GC.A5Programs`. The group $A_5$ is
`alternatingGroup (Fin 5)` (points $1,\dots,5$ of the papers are `0,…,4`); concrete permutations
are written by their value vectors and all finite checks are decided by the kernel (`decide`,
`decide +kernel`; no `native_decide`). Straight-line programs are lists of operations (`leaf`,
`mul i j`, `inv i`) whose operands are indices of earlier nodes, so sharing is explicit and the
size of a program is its length. Complexity statements (P-completeness, NC, logspace computability
of reductions, work and depth bounds, PRAM algorithms) are not formalized; what is formalized is the
mathematical content behind them, as listed below.

**004a (pigeonhole collisions).** The Maurer–Rhodes theorem is proved in full generality: every
function $G^k\to G$ of a finite simple nonabelian group is given by a group term with constants.
From it: the 61-probe selector for $A_5$ (61 fixed unary terms whose values at any $x$ contain
exactly one equal pair, $\{0, j+1\}$ for $x=g_j$; Theorem 3.2), the fact that any colliding pair
determines $x$ (core of Corollary 3.4), and predicate-complete equality (Theorem 3.5). The
universal-collision count is formalized: $N$ nodes with value tables over a context set $X$ contain
at least $(N-60^{|X|})/2$ disjoint pairs equal in every context (Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3). The
value/equality calculus (R1)–(R6) is defined as an inductive predicate and proved sound and complete
for well-formed programs (Theorem 5.1). The Boolean gadget lemma (Lemma 2.2) is formalized as a
compilation of Boolean circuits into $A_5$ multiplication circuits of size at most $17s$ and then,
via the two-generator normal form, into constant-free programs over $P\mapsto p$, $Q\mapsto q$. Not
formalized: the P-hardness claims as complexity statements, the depth bounds of ranked saturation
(Theorem 6.2), the spine lemma, component collapse (Theorem 6.4) and the conditional surplus law
(Section 7).

**004b (invisible to solvable groups).** Every element of $A_5$ is a commutator (explicit table).
The commutator tower $R_{g,d}$ is defined in the free group $F(P,Q)$ and proved to evaluate to $g$
and to lie in $F^{(d)}$ (Theorem 4.2(i),(ii)); substituting it for the constants of an $A_5$ circuit
keeps every value and puts every node into $F^{(d)}$ (Theorem 5.1, semantic part). Proved in general:
$G^{(d)}\le\gamma_{2^d}(G)$ (Lemma 2.1, via a three-subgroup lemma modulo a normal subgroup), shadow
annihilation for solvable images of derived length $\le d$ and nilpotent images of class $<2^d$
(Theorem 6.1), and that a finite solvable group of order $<2^d$ has trivial $d$-th derived subgroup
(Corollary 6.2, Remark 6.3(1)). The two-generator normal form (Theorem 3.1) is formalized with a
library of 60 nodes (size $s+60$). Not formalized: the node count $64+240d$ of the shared tower as an
explicit program (Theorem 4.2(iii)), logspace constructibility, and the P-completeness of
$\mathrm{Deep}_c$ (Theorem 5.2) as a complexity statement.

**004c (nineteen orbits).** P. Hall's theorem is proved in general: if $T$ is simple nonabelian and
generating pairs $(x_i,y_i)$ are pairwise not related by an automorphism, then $(x_i)_i,(y_i)_i$
generate $T^\iota$ (Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.2). For $A_5$, a kernel-checked certificate for each of the
3600 pairs shows: a pair generates $A_5$ iff it is conjugate under $S_5$ to one of 19 explicit
representatives, by a unique permutation; otherwise it lies in one of 21 explicit maximal subgroups,
each metabelian. Hence exactly 2280 generating and 1320 non-generating pairs, forming 19 free
$S_5$-orbits; distinct representatives are separated by automorphism invariants, so evaluation on
them maps $F_2$ onto $A_5^{19}$. The mirror-word theorem (Theorem 6.1), the 19 selectors
(Corollary 6.2) and non-interpolation (Corollary 6.3, without the size bound $m+O(1)$) are proved.
Also proved: power permutations and unit-power reversibility (Lemma 3.5, Theorem 3.6) and the
inverse of the Fibonacci update (Corollary 3.3). Not formalized: that $\mathrm{Aut}(A_5)$ is $S_5$
(the orbit statements use $S_5$-conjugation), the census by subgroup order, the Fibonacci cycle
structure, and the scan, fusion and cold-sector compilers (Theorems 3.2, 3.8, 4.1).

**004d (one entry vs two entries).** One-entry programs over any group compute powers $x^{a_v}$ with
exponents given by the local rules (Theorem 3.1); over $A_5$ the exponents reduce mod 30, giving at
most 30 value functions (Corollary 3.2). Dominator condensation (Theorem 4.3, main claim): if $d$
dominates $v$ in a well-formed program then $\mathrm{val}(v)=\mathrm{val}(d)^a$ with $a$ independent
of the entry values; inverse and squaring gates are dominated by their operand (Theorem 4.3(iii)).
Theorem 6.1 is formalized as the power normal form plus the embedding of Boolean circuits into
constant-free two-entry programs. Not formalized: Theorem 4.3(iv) and prime roots, the prime-core
construction (Theorem 4.4), path counting, and all NC² / depth statements.

**004e (few disjoint cycles).** The descriptor algebra (Lemma 5.1), the cycle monodromy bijection
between solutions of a cycle of relations and roots of the return map (Theorem 5.2), and the full
$A_5$ root-domain classification (Theorem 5.3: $|D_1(A,B)|\in\{0,3,4,5,60\}$, nonzero iff $A$ is
conjugate to $B^{-1}$, then equal to $|C(B)|$, 60 only for $A=B=e$; $|D_{-1}(A,B)|\in\{0,1,16\}$,
16 iff $A=B$, 0 iff $A^{-1}B$ has order 2). Not formalized: the stress family (Theorem 4.1), the
2-core lemma, the short-kernel-cycle lemma and the cycle-packing feedback set (Lemmas 3.1–3.2,
Theorem 3.3), and the compilers and depth bounds.

**004f (opening A5 cycles).** The hard-coefficient cycle (Theorem 2.1) and its link to Boolean
circuits through the $A_5$ compilation (core of Corollary 2.2); the mixer cycle and mixer inverse
(Lemma 3.1); bijectivity of mixer strips and the impossibility of a summary by fewer than $W$ group
values (Theorem 3.2(i),(iii)); the Nielsen-strip embedding of multiplication circuits with $s$
registers and at most $2s$ transvections $g_i\leftarrow g_ig_j$, $i\ne j$ (Theorem 7.2). Symbolic
opening (Theorem 3.3) is covered by the monodromy and descriptor-composition theorems of 004e. Not
formalized: Theorem 3.2(ii) (disjoint cycles in the graphs), ancestrally liftable kernels, and the
transfer statements.

**004g (Barrington gadgets and Strahler rank).** The gadget identities (Lemma 3.2), NOT/AND
correctness with the multiplication-only AND (Theorem 3.3), and the linear Boolean-to-$A_5$
reduction with sharing preserved (Corollary 3.4). For circuits given by predecessor lists, the
Strahler rank satisfies $2^{\mathrm{sr}(v)}\le U(v)$ (Corollary 6.8, with the identity congruence and
no designated constants; $U$ counts unfolding nodes over distinct predecessors) and is monotone along
arcs; the quotient lemma (Lemma 6.1) is proved for straight-line programs. Not formalized: the
transfer theorem (Theorem 4.1), portal chains, the rank computation and the depth of the compiler.

**004h (stable colors).** The Frucht graph (LCF code above) is cubic and asymmetric; on its colored
incidence graph one refinement round keeps the two classes, while the only color-preserving
automorphism is the identity (Theorem 3.1). Rotations of $D^q$ are transitive on positions and have at
least $|D|^q/q$ orbits (Theorem 4.1, lower bound via Burnside's lemma; the exact necklace formula is
not formalized). The symmetric shell with an $A_5$ selector (existing by Maurer–Rhodes) is
$S_q$-equivariant and its response is constant $e$ or the identity according to $g$, so it decides a
compiled Boolean circuit (Theorem 5.1). Equivariance under transpositions along a connected graph
implies full $S_q$-equivariance (Theorem 6.4, connected case). Not formalized: Lemma 6.3 (vertex
permutations as certificates), the component version of Theorem 6.4, and the orbit-table and
swap-wreath compilers.
