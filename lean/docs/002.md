# Newton's method over GF(2) and the Strahler rank

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [Newton's Method Evaluates Circuits in Strahler-Rank Rounds](../../preprints/Newtons-Method-Evaluates-Circuits-in-Strahler-Rank-Rounds-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)
- [Horn Saturation in Strahler-Rank Reachability Rounds](../../preprints/Horn-Saturation-in-Strahler-Rank-Reachability-Rounds-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)

## Scope

**002a (Newton's method).** An affine–product circuit over $\mathbb F_2 = $ `ZMod 2` is a list of constant gates, affine gates $x_i = c_i + \sum_{j\in A(i)} x_j$ and product gates $x_i = x_{p(i)}x_{q(i)}$ with $p(i)\ne q(i)$, all parents preceding the gate. The Lean development defines the residual $F$, the formal Jacobian $J(x)$, the exact Newton map $N(x) = x + J(x)^{-1}F(x)$ (with Mathlib's matrix inverse), and the Horton–Strahler rank $\rho$ by the recursion of Definition 3.2. It proves that $J(x)$ is unit lower triangular ($\det J(x) = 1$), that the value vector exists and is unique, that $N(x) = x$ exactly at the value vector, the exact error identity $J(x)e^+ = Q(e)$ (Lemma 3.1), and the Newton–Strahler theorem (Theorem 3.4): from any start, after $t$ steps every gate of rank $< t$ is exact, so $\rho(C)+1$ steps give the value vector. It also proves the valid-layering form of Remark 3.5(ii) (the rank is the smallest valid layering, and any valid layering bounded by $K$ certifies $K+1$ rounds). For gates of higher degree (Section 6), it defines triangular Boolean systems with arbitrary gate functions, the multilinear derivative $\partial_S$ (as the $\mathbb F_2$ finite difference $\sum_{T\subseteq S} g(x_S := 1_T)$, which is the formal derivative of the algebraic normal form; this identification is standard and is not itself proved in Lean), the Taylor remainder and the corrected Taylor rank of Definition 6.3, and proves the multilinear Taylor identity (Lemma 6.1), the exact error identity $J(x)e^+ = R(x,e)$ (Theorem 6.2) and the Taylor-rank contraction (Theorem 6.5). Affine gates with an empty input set are allowed (they are constant gates). The depth and size accounting (Lemma 4.1, Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3, Theorem 7.4) is not formalized; neither are the affine–product normal form (Lemma 2.2), the derivation-tree characterization of the rank (Proposition 3.7), the reduction to the integer Newton map (Remark 3.8), the multiplexer lemmas of Section 7, the examples of Section 5 and the rank-frontier quotient of Section 8.

**002b (Horn saturation).** A Horn system has a set of axioms and a finite list of rules $P\Rightarrow q$ with a finite set of premises. Derivation trees are an inductive family, and the rank of a tree is the Horton–Strahler combination of Definition 2.2 (the largest child rank, plus one if it is attained by two distinct premises). With $S_k$ the facts having a tree of rank at most $k$ and $S_{-1} = \emptyset$, the formalization proves the exact layer theorem (Theorem 4.1): $S_k$ is the set of facts reachable from the seeds $B_k$ in the graph $G_k$, both built from $S_{k-1}$; consequently the explicitly defined saturation iteration (start from $\emptyset$, one reachability layer per round) gives exactly $S_K$ after $K+1$ rounds. It also proves the stabilization corollary (Corollary 4.2, including $\sigma(q)\le|\mathcal F|-1$ for every derivable fact over a finite set of facts), the identification of the layers with the Boolean Newton operator $N(X) = Df_X^*(f(X))$ (Proposition 6.1, $S_k = N^{k+1}(\emptyset)$), the leaf bound $2^{\mathrm{rank}}\le\#\text{leaves}$ (Lemma 2.3(b)) and the round part of the few-leaves corollary (Corollary 5.3), and the truth-table lemma (Lemma 7.2): the truth-table system of a Boolean circuit of fan-in at most two derives exactly the true facts, each of proof rank at most the depth of its gate. The circuit depth and size bounds of the saturation algorithm (Theorem 5.1) and the equivalence of saturation rank with parallel depth (Theorem 7.3) are complexity statements and are not formalized; only their mathematical cores (the round count and Lemma 7.2) are. The examples of Section 3 are not formalized.
