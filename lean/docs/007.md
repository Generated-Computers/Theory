# Structural compilers and their limits

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [Parameterized NC Compilers for a P-Complete Problem](../../preprints/Parameterized-NC-Compilers-for-a-P-Complete-Problem-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)
- [Segment Cuts in NC² and a Unique-Games Barrier](../../preprints/Segment-Cuts-in-NC2-and-a-Unique-Games-Barrier-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)
- [A Pebbling Barrier for Clone-and-Decompose Algorithms](../../preprints/A-Pebbling-Barrier-for-Clone-and-Decompose-Algorithms-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)
- [Few Union Profiles Put Circuit Evaluation in NC²](../../preprints/Few-Union-Profiles-Put-Circuit-Evaluation-in-NC2-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)

## Scope

*Parameterized NC compilers (007a).* For a Sequential NOR parent array and a horizon `n`, the formalization proves that the true reset mask is the unique fixed point of the map `Φ_j` (Lemma 3.2), and the greedy phase-repair theorem (Theorem 3.6): if the error of a proposal has `k` switches on `1, …, n`, then after normalizing the first bit, `k` rounds of `T_j` give the true mask and no earlier round does. For a normalized binary core it proves that the interaction cover `C(j)` meets every binary record (Lemma 4.5, for the paper's side rule and for every other side rule), that relative to the cover values every record outside the cover is a unary map of one earlier non-cover variable or of the virtual root, determined by cover values below the gate (Lemma 4.5), and the corrected residual evaluator of Theorem 4.6: every residual value `y_k(X)` equals the ordered ancestry product `h_{k,m} ∘ ⋯ ∘ h_{k,1}` applied to `1`. It also checks the counterexample of Remark 4.7: on the two-stage chain, `y₂ = ¬c₁ ∧ ¬c₂` is not a constant or a signed copy of a single variable. The binary-core compression, the block-lifting lemma, the circuit size and depth bounds, and all other compilers of the paper (core depth, formula cores, sharing, interface tables, treewidth, repair, live intervals, elimination orders) are not formalized. The paper proves the second clause of Lemma 4.5 ("depends on at most one value in `C(j)`") as stated, but it fails when both sources of a gate outside the cover lie in the cover. The Lean statement says "cover values below `k`" instead, which agrees with the "at most two values" used in the proof of Theorem 4.6.

*Segment cuts and the Unique-Games barrier (007b).* The formalization proves the maximal-packing bound of Theorem 6.4 in hypergraph form: the union of a maximal family of pairwise disjoint `(L+1)`-sets meets every member, and its size is at most `(L+1)` times the size of any hitting set. It defines the guard–router expansion `R_d(H)` of Definition 7.3 without the virtual sink, and proves Theorem 7.5 (with the path lifting and core counting of Lemma 7.4) in both directions: the cores of a feasible set of `H` for length `k` are feasible for `K = k(2d+1)`, and the owners of a feasible set of `R_d(H)` for length `K` are feasible for `k`. Hence `dvd_K(R_d(H)) = dvd_k(H)`. This holds for every digraph, every `d ≥ 1`, every assignment of length-`d` words to the arcs, and every `k`. On the family `𝒩_k` of Theorem 8.6 it proves `scut_1 = dvd_2 = k + 1`, and that every feasible union of the classes `A` and `B` has at least `2k + 1` vertices. The UGC-conditional barrier (Svensson's theorem, quoted as Theorem 7.2, and Corollaries 7.7 and 7.9), the skeleton realization lemma (Lemma 7.8), the canonical expansion, the compilers, the NC² path-packing algorithm, the rank schedules, and the computation of the rank tuples of `𝒩_k` are not formalized.

*Pebbling barrier (007c).* The formalization defines black pebblings (a step places a pebble on a vertex whose predecessors are pebbled, removes a pebble, or repeats the configuration) and predecessor-preserving partial unfoldings of gate DAGs. It proves the pebbling projection lemma (Lemma 4.4: every pebbling of an unfolding projects to a pebbling of the circuit of no larger cost) and that every vertex of an unfolding computes the value of its label. It also proves the grid pebbling price `Peb(Q_g) = g + 1` for `g ≥ 2` (Lemma 4.8), with the lower bound by the pyramid blocking argument and the upper bound by the explicit row-by-row strategy. As a consequence, every pebbling of every predecessor-preserving partial unfolding of `C_g` costs at least `g + 1`. The tree-depth pebbling bound (Lemma 4.5), the treewidth/tree-depth inequality (Lemma 4.6), and therefore the treewidth form of Theorem 4.7 and Corollaries 4.9–4.10 are not formalized. Neither are the four-coordinate embedding, the tail-factorized system of Section 3, and the compilers.

*Few union profiles (007d).* The formalization proves absorbing elimination (Lemma 2.2) for tagged absorbing systems with arbitrary exception values. For exception-port systems it proves the composition lemma of the union-profile compiler (Lemma 3.2): both child inputs lie in the union closures, the child runs reproduce the run of the parent, and the response identity `ρ_I(α) = u_L|_{after I} ∨ u_{R'}` holds. It also proves that the root run on `0` is the true computation, and that the true contribution vector at every interval lies in `𝒰(A^in_I)` and reproduces the true values. It proves the three union-closure bounds of Proposition 4.1 and the matching example (`|𝒰(I_w)| = 2^w`, Example 4.3). The capped closure enumeration, the circuit construction of Theorem 3.3 and all depth bounds, the reduction to port systems as a circuit, and the nested example are not formalized.

No statement about NC, logspace uniformity or circuit depth is formalized.
