# Stable semisort and P versus EP

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [Stable Semisort Is Exactly as Hard as Padded Sorting](../../preprints/Stable-Semisort-Is-Exactly-as-Hard-as-Padded-Sorting-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)
- [Exact Cut Contraction for P-Complete Sequential Circuits](../../preprints/Exact-Cut-Contraction-for-P-Complete-Sequential-Circuits-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)

## Scope

**Stable semisort and padded sorting (011a).** Inputs are `n` records identified by their positions, outputs are lists of original indices, and the problems of Definitions 2.1–2.2 (unstable semisort, stable semisort, stable integer sort, sorting distinct keys, padded sorting) are stated as output specifications. The black boxes are modeled as arbitrary valid outputs, or as oracles that solve every valid instance; nothing is assumed about how they work. Both directions of the equivalence are formalized as explicit transformations with correctness proofs. (1) Theorem 3.1: from any valid unstable-semisort output, the run marks, run numbers, group identifiers and composite keys `(n+1) gid[i] + i` of stages S1–S4 form a valid `PaddedSort(n,(n+1)^2)` instance, and the compaction of any valid padded output is a stable semisort. This includes Lemmas 3.2–3.5, Lemma 2.4 and the reductions `IS ⪯ DS` and `SS ⪯ IS + USemi` of Theorem 4.1. (2) The converse, `PaddedSort(n,(n+1)^2) ⪯ SS`, makes no call to an unstable semisort. It composes `DS ⪯ IS` (a two-pass radix sort; the second pass sorts the records in the order of the first) with `IS ⪯ SS` (segmented-scan offsets, group lengths, their prefix sums and one exclusive scatter) and Lemma 2.4. The collision-energy identity (Lemma 6.2) and the density barrier (Theorem 7.3) are also formalized. Not formalized: all costs (work, depth, space, CREW exclusivity of writes, failure probabilities), so Corollaries 3.7 and 4.3, which are statements about costs, are not formalized either. In `IS ⪯ SS` the group lengths are defined as counts over the semisorted list, not computed by a scan. Also not formalized: the colored-prefix-rank reductions of Theorem 4.1(d), the counting-pass and radix-restoration bounds of Section 5, and the random-anchor bounds of Section 7 (Proposition 7.1, Theorem 7.2).

**Exact cut contraction (011b).** Binary-table sequential circuits (Definition 2.2) are formalized with values defined by recursion. Theorem 4.1 (exact cut externalization) is formalized in full as a correctness statement. For a cut `b` and given prefix values `x_0, …, x_b`, the explicitly constructed contracted circuit `C/b` (one gate per internal gate, parents `π'_r = ρ(π_{h_r})`, tables `G'_r(u,v) = G_{h_r}(Φ_{h_r-1}(u), Φ_{π_{h_r}}(v))`) has a valid shape on `q(b)` gates, and its values satisfy `y_r = x_{h_r}`. The suffix values are recovered as `x_i = Φ_i(y_{ρ(i)})` for every `i ∈ [b,n]`, so they are determined by the contracted instance. Also formalized: the enumeration of internal gates and `q(b) = |{i > b : π_i > b}|`; the crossing-area identity and its averaging consequence (Lemma 5.2); and the padding counterexample (Proposition 5.3), the finite statement that refutes the source's claim that quadratic total edge span gives an efficient parallel regime. The latter says that `C'` followed by `m` gates reading `x_0` has total span at least `n²/4`, a top cut with `q = 0` and `(b/n)^γ + (q/n)^γ = 2^{-γ} < 1`, and `C'` as its prefix. Its Sequential NOR version is included. Proposition 3.1 (Sequential NOR equals the lexicographically-first greedy maximal independent set, stated as the unique solution of the greedy recursion) and Lemma 3.3 (jump rule) are also formalized. Not formalized: all costs (the `O(n)`-work, `O(log n)`-depth construction, the `O(1)`-depth recovery), the cut-norm recursion (Theorem 5.1) and Corollary 5.4, which are statements about work and depth, and Section 6 (register stack machines, linear algebraic circuits, elimination rounds, Conjecture 6.7). No statement about P, EP or NC is formalized.
