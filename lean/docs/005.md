## Scope

**005a (Separator speedups).** The formalization covers the combinatorics of the balanced interval tree on the stages `1, …, N`. It proves that the tree has height `⌈log₂ N⌉`, and it proves the constant-lag lemma: every node is assigned at most `c` of the edges `(s, s + c)`. It also proves the congestion lemma, the linear separator budget `∑_v |E_v| ≤ N - 1` and the bound `#heavy · B < N`. The dyadic-to-chronological frontier lemma is proved: in an all-low subtree of height `h`, every cut is crossed by at most `B h` edges. For the online separator transfer, the formalization proves the counting part. The recursion `Solve`, defined explicitly, makes at most `1 + 2 · #heavy · ⌈log₂ N⌉` calls whenever the low-subtree algorithm fails only at marked nodes. The padding definition, the padding-collapse theorem and the calibration corollary are not formalized. The same holds for the depth bounds and every complexity-class conclusion (MCP ∈ NC, P ⊆ SP).

**005b (Every hypergraph is an A5 proof-support system).** Collision blocks, essential support and edge products are defined for an arbitrary group. The 61-probe selector lemma is proved for `A₅`, together with its analogue with `|G| + 1` probes for every finite group. The explicit selector uses only the identity and constant maps, so each probe is visibly a group term. The realization theorem is proved for every selector and every nontrivial group. Each block has exactly one colliding pair, that pair determines the edge product `a_e`, and the essential support of the block's collision function is exactly the edge `e`. Part (i) of the star-transversal barrier is also proved: as one anchor varies, the colliding pair takes `|G|` (that is, 60) distinct values. The charging lemma is proved over the reals, including both the phase count and the depth bound `λ n^{1-ε}/(1-ε)`. The following are not formalized: the size count of the construction, the support-redundancy proposition, parts (ii) and (iii) of the star barrier, the surplus transfer, and all complexity statements. Terms are represented by the functions they induce.

**005c (Exponential-state portals).** For `M(x, y) = e` if `x = e` and `y` otherwise, the formalization proves two facts. The output vectors cover all of `G^t`, and the hard-coefficient capacity switch holds: the image has one element if `x = e`, and otherwise `|G|^t` elements (`60^t` for `A₅`). For ranked Horn systems, the rank-layer map `Φ(X) = Reach_{G(X)}(X ∪ Seed(X))` is proved monotone and to satisfy `Φ(𝓕_{<k}) = 𝓕_{≤k}` for `k ≥ 1`. The band corollary `Φ^B(𝓕_{<k}) = 𝓕_{≤k+B-1}` is also proved. A two-fact example confirms the paper's correction that the source's newly-derivable map is not monotone. The stopping rule of the one-shot criterion is proved: the first rank `K` with `min_{K' ≤ K}(K' + c_{K'}) ≤ K` is at most `min_K (K + c_K)`. Circuit size and depth, the symbolic and branching-program portal theorems, the composition-algebra theorem and the fanout-kernel compiler's depth bound are not formalized. The realization of `M` as an `A₅` term (Maurer–Rhodes) is not formalized either.

**005d (Menger's theorem inside one Strahler layer).** Ranked Horn systems are defined with Horton–Strahler proof ranks. The rank-layer reachability lemma `𝓕_{≤k} = 𝓕_{<k} ∪ Reach_{G_k}(S_k)` (`k ≥ 1`) is proved in full, including the case that the source's proof skipped. The closure lemma `D ∩ R = lfp_R(D ∩ H)` is proved for every closed boundary `H` of a region `R`, with `lfp_R` defined as the least closed set. The side-premise star theorem is proved for its derivability pattern: the conclusions `C_i` are derivable exactly for the `i` whose `B_i` is derivable, so all `2^t` patterns occur. Menger's theorem is not in Mathlib, so the unary-spine Menger portal and its corollary are not formalized. Also not formalized are the forest-mass lemma, the `A₅` realization of the star and the portal compilation and complexity theorems.

**005e (Fanout height).** Programs are modelled as directed graphs with a fanout set `H`, and paths are lists. The round structure of the fanout-height compiler is formalized as an explicit iteration. In round `j + 1`, a node is computed once all of its skeleton predecessors are known. If every path carries at most `d` fanout nodes, every node is known after `d + 1` rounds. On acyclic graphs a path carries at most `|H|` fanout nodes. For Sequential NOR, the proposition `H = {j₁, …, jₙ}` and `d(C) = h` is proved. The per-round formula evaluation and its depth are not formalized, and neither is the equality-assisted redirection. The tensor-network section is not formalized: copy-context normal form, Hopf identities, copy-tensor rank and low-copy-debt compiler.

**005f (Control observability).** The affine history machine over `ℚ` is defined as an explicit step function. Invalid addresses read `0`, a convention that normalized executions never use. The control-observability closure (C1)–(C3), demanded reads and control-live cells are defined. The theorem "control-observable state suffices" is proved in its two-execution form. Take two executions that agree at a cut `τ` on the control state, on the functionals of `L_τ`, on the external history and on the control-live cells. They then have the same addresses, branch bits and control states for the rest of the interval. For the balanced ladders `D_{r,w}`, the formalization proves three facts. Every vertex of layer `k` has Strahler rank `k`, its two predecessors are distinct, and there are exactly `2rw` edges. The linear separator budget and the counting part of the separator transfer are the 005a theorems. The least-closure lemma, the asymptotic corollary on `N/log²N` and all complexity statements are not formalized.

**005g (Batching first failures).** Local machines are formalized as step functions whose value at step `t` depends only on earlier configurations. The first-failure validator lemma is proved: the least local-check failure is the first-failure boundary, and the recomputed successor there is exact. The same-anchor batch collapse is proved as a set identity: the steps certified by a family of candidates equal those certified by the candidate with the largest first-failure boundary. The black-box two-round barrier is proved: no one-round algorithm with fewer than `|Y|` non-adaptive queries computes `R(L(x))` for all oracles. The round count of the low-junction transducer is the generic round lemma of 005e, applied with the junctions as `H`. Not formalized: the depth and work bounds, the parametric cut composition, and the conditional corollaries.
