# Random and smoothed Sequential NOR

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [A P-Complete Problem in Average-Case NC](../../preprints/A-P-Complete-Problem-in-Average-Case-NC-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)
- [A Gap Theorem for P versus BPNC](../../preprints/A-Gap-Theorem-for-P-versus-BPNC-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)
- [A P-Complete Problem in Average-Case AC1 and SC2](../../preprints/A-P-Complete-Problem-in-Average-Case-AC1-and-SC2-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)
- [Small Noise Defeats Two Natural Parallel Iterations](../../preprints/Small-Noise-Defeats-Two-Natural-Parallel-Iterations-November-3-2026/paper.pdf)

## Scope

All four papers concern Sequential NOR, $x_0=1$, $x_i=\mathrm{NOR}(x_{i-1},x_{j_i})$ with $j_i<i$. Instances are formalized as infinite pointer sequences. A length-$n$ instance is the restriction to $j_1,\dots,j_n$, and the formalization proves that traces and Newton iterates on $[0,n]$ depend only on these pointers. Values are in $\mathbb{Z}/2$. No complexity class (NC, AC$^1$, SC$^2$, BPNC) is formalized. Nothing about random instances or about probabilities of events over the noise is formalized either, except two exact finite identities in the second paper.

*A P-Complete Problem in Average-Case NC (001a).* Newton's method over GF(2) is defined explicitly, by forward substitution of the linearised system with the paper's NOT-gate convention. The formalization proves the following.
- Lemma 4.2, the error recursion, at NOT gates, binary gates and position $0$.
- The trace is a fixed point of the Newton map.
- Lemma 4.3, the front identity. The next error vanishes up to $i$ when there is no source up to $i$, it equals $1$ at the first source, and $f_{t+1}\ge f_t+2$.
- The deterministic part of Lemma 4.4: iterates on $[0,n]$ depend only on $j_1,\dots,j_n$.
- Lemma 5.2(i), the pathwise witness property.
- Proposition 3.1, the Newton–Strahler bound. After $t$ rounds every gate of Horton–Strahler rank below $t$ is exact, from any start, so $T_n\le\rho(n)+1$.
- Proposition 8.1 in full, Newton's exact worst case. From the standard start $T_n\le\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ for all instances, and the explicit instance $W$ has front exactly $f_t=2t+3$ and $T_n(W)=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ for every $n\ge3$. So the maximum over all instances is exactly $\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$.
- Corollary 8.5: the lexicographically first maximal independent set of the graph with edges $\{i-1,i\}$, $\{j_i,i\}$ is the set of ones of the trace.
- The final algebraic step of Theorem 8.3: if $L\le U$ lie in $[0,1/2]$ with $L\ge g(U)$ and $U\le g(L)$, where $g(u)=(1-u)/(2-u)$, then $L=U=(3-\sqrt5)/2$.

The main probabilistic results are not formalized: the $O(\log n)$ bound with probability $1-o(1)$ (Theorem 7.1, Theorem A), the lower bound $\log_2\ln n-3$ (Theorem 5.3), the Strahler asymptotics (Theorem 3.2), the random-structure bounds (Proposition 8.2), the existence of the almost-sure density limit, and the NC/space consequences (Corollaries 7.3 and 8.4). Lemmas 4.1, 4.5, 4.6 and the window lemma are not formalized.

*A Gap Theorem for P versus BPNC (001b).* The formalization proves the following.
- Lemma 3.3, the composition of smoothings, as an exact identity of finite laws. It is proved for one coordinate and for the product law on parent arrays of length $n$, together with the up-set step ($\varepsilon\le\varepsilon''\le1$ gives a suitable $\varepsilon'\in[0,1]$).
- Lemma 3.4, the gadgets $N$ and $M$, and the 6-gate majority gadget $M'$ with $c=k-1$, for every instance carrying the listed pointers and whatever $x^*_{k-1}$.
- The density facts behind Lemma 3.6: an intact $N$ has a one, and an intact $M'$ has at least two ones among its six positions.
- The compiler Comp, defined for cells of every depth $h$ (Theorem 3.5 uses $h=3$) with an arbitrary NOR circuit and arbitrary coins. It is shown to be a valid parent array.
- Exactness at $\varepsilon=0$: $x^*_{o(t,\ell)}=\mathrm{val}(g_t)$ for all $t,\ell$ and all coins.
- Step 1 of Theorem 3.5 (dominating events), pathwise for every instance. If a cell's output is wrong, the event $D(\mathrm{root})$ holds, with "hit" replaced by the weaker "does not carry its listed pointers".
- The numbers of Step 3 of Theorem 3.5 ($q_0\le0.204$, $q_1\le0.11587$, $q_2\le0.04517$, $q_3\le0.01394$ for $\varepsilon\le1/1000$, $\beta\le1/10$) and the constant $10e\bar q<0.38$ of Step 4.
- The ten-iteration bound $q_{10}\le0.1161$ of Theorem 3.7.

The following are not formalized: the probabilistic Steps 2, 4 and 5 (conditional independence, binomial and Chernoff tails), Theorems 3.5 and 3.7 themselves, Lemma 3.6, the compiler Comp$'$ (padding, depth-10 cells with $M'$, flags), the logspace computability of the compiler, Corollaries 3.8–3.10 and the gap Theorem 3.11. These are conditional statements about P and BPNC.

*A P-Complete Problem in Average-Case AC1 and SC2 (001c).* The formalization proves the following.
- The reset decomposition $x^*_i=x^*_{\varrho(i)}\oplus((i-\varrho(i))\bmod2)$.
- The Kleene–prefix iteration of Definition 3.1, defined explicitly. Lemma 3.2 is proved in full: $K_t=\{i:d(i)\le t\}$, and the recorded values are the trace. Hence the iteration succeeds after $R$ rounds exactly when $\max d\le R$.
- Dependency chains, with the deterministic first step of Theorem 3.4. If $d(i)\ge L+1$ there is a chain of length $L$ along which the depth drops by one per step. So, if no chain of length $L$ stays at positions $\ge g_0\ge1$, the iteration knows every position after $L+g_0-1$ rounds.
- The threshold algebra. Admissible $\eta,\lambda$ with $\theta<1$ exist iff $\varepsilon>(\sqrt{17}-1)/4$ (Theorem 3.4). The limit equation of Lemma 3.5 gives $\varepsilon\ell^*=(2+\varepsilon-\sqrt{4+4\varepsilon-3\varepsilon^2})/2$. Also $\varepsilon\ell^*>1-\varepsilon$ iff $\varepsilon>(1+\sqrt{13})/6$ (Theorem 3.6), and $\varepsilon\varpi(\varepsilon)>1-\varepsilon$ iff $\varepsilon>3/4$, with $\varpi(3/4)=1/3$ (Theorem 3.9).

The relevance-pruned iteration and Lemmas 3.11 and 3.12 are formalized, in the file of the fourth paper (see below). The following are not formalized: the density lemmas (3.3, 3.5 beyond the limit equation, 3.8), the probability bounds of Theorems 3.4, 3.6 and 3.9 (Steps 2–5), the AC$^0$ implementability of a round, and Corollaries 3.15–3.16 (average-case AC$^1$ and SC$^2$, $\varepsilon_c\le3/4$).

*Small Noise Defeats Two Natural Parallel Iterations (001d).* The formalization proves the following.
- The relevance-pruned iteration of Definition 3.3 and its depth $D$, defined by the paper's min–max formula over anchors.
- Lemma 3.4 in full: (a) $K_t=\{i:D(i)\le t\}$ with correct values, and (b) every position known to the Kleene–prefix iteration is known to the pruned one, via $D\le d$.
- Lemma 3.5 in full, the switch characterisation. Part (a) gives the recursions for $D$ (four cases) and for $d$ (three cases). Part (b) gives the characterisation of $D(i)\ge t+1$ and of $d(i)\ge t+1$.
- Lemma 4.2 in full (non-deep positions), for $D$ and for $d$, including the claims about $x^*_p=1$ and about gate $u+1$.
- The deterministic over-approximation step of Theorem 4.3: Lemma 4.2 read through abstract indicators $\hat U$ and $\hat K$ gives conditions (A$'$) and (B$'$), for both parts.
- The exact numbers used in that proof:
  - the bound $\sum_{r<R}N(r)\le(2-u)/(1-u)^2$ for the recursion $a_r,b_r$;
  - $u^*$, $k^*$, $\mu$ and $\mu_1$ and the inequalities $\mu\le\sigma^*-\eta$ and $\mu_1\le\sigma^1_*-\eta$ in both parts;
  - $u^*(2-u^*)\in\{7/16,231/400\}$ with $6\ln(1/(u^*(2-u^*)))\ge3$;
  - $2\eta^2m\ge6\ln n$.

The random block bases and Theorem 4.3 itself (the lower bound $n/(5\cdot10^6\ln^2n)$ with probability $1-n^{-1/2}$) are not formalized. Neither are their probabilistic steps: the conditional laws, the monotone coupling, the Hoeffding bounds, and the parameter estimates involving $\ln n$ and ceilings.
